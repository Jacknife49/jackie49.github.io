## Performative reinforcement learning

Algorithms often influence the environments in which they operate. Their decisions can shift the distribution of future data, creating feedback between the model and its environment. This interaction is studied through performative prediction and performative learning.

My research connects these ideas to sequential decision-making using infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision processes. I am particularly interested in the consequences of policy-induced environmental changes for learning and decision-making over time.

## Long-term trustworthy machine learning

Fairness criteria evaluated on a fixed distribution may fail to account for the consequences of deploying an algorithm. My doctoral work aims to bridge long-term fairness and performative learning, including connections to differential privacy and other safeguards for trustworthy machine learning.

## Graph property testing

I began working on graph property testing during my Project Research Fellowship at ACMU, ISI Kolkata. This is a parallel line of research that reflects my broader interest in graph theory and theoretical computer science.

## Mathematical foundations

My interests are rooted in measure-theoretic probability, convex geometry, and optimization, with an interest in functional analysis. These perspectives guide my work in statistical learning theory, online learning, and sequential decision-making.
